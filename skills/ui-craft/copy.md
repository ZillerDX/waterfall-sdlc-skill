# UI copy

Words are part of the design. Each element does exactly one job.

- **User's vocabulary, not the system's.** "Notifications", not "webhook config". Name things by what people recognize.
- **Say what it is or does**, plainly. Describe, don't sell. Specific beats clever.
- **Buttons say what happens:** "Save changes", "Place order", "Export CSV" — not "Submit", "OK", "Continue" when a specific verb exists.
- **Same action, same word** through the whole flow: button "Publish" → toast "Published" → history "Published by…".
- **Errors:** what happened + how to fix, in the interface's voice. No apology, never vague. "Card expired — use another card", not "Something went wrong".
- **Empty states:** an invitation to act. "No trades yet. Connect a broker to import history." + one button.
- **Sentence case**, plain verbs, no filler, no exclamation marks in system messages.
- **Real content in designs.** Realistic names, numbers, and lengths (including long ones) so truncation and density are tested. Never lorem ipsum.

## Thai
- Use the verbs Thai users already see in apps: บันทึก, ยกเลิก, ลบ, แก้ไข, ส่ง, ดาวน์โหลด, ค้นหา.
- Keep established loanwords (อีเมล, ล็อกอิน, แดชบอร์ด) when they're what users say; don't coin formal Thai terms nobody uses.
- No ครับ/ค่ะ in labels, buttons, or errors; allowed sparingly in onboarding or support copy if the brand voice is friendly.
- Errors specific and calm: "ยังไม่ได้กรอกอีเมล", "รหัสผ่านต้องมีอย่างน้อย 8 ตัวอักษร" — not "เกิดข้อผิดพลาด กรุณาลองใหม่".
- Avoid the Thai slogan tells listed in `generic-tells.md`.
